Different Endings

Cabaret is a very fluid show. Given the different interpretations on the Emcee, there are bound to be different endings based on what message is trying to be conveyed.

For the original show, the ending included the majority of the cast standing still, with a large mirror being lowered onto the stage. Nobody from the cast moves to stop it or get off the stage. The mirror is supposed to reflect on the audience and how saying "it's just politics" affects everyone. When fascism takes over, it takes over everyone.

Another ending invloves a more sinister path with the Emcee in a Nazi uniform and gas mask, leading the rest of the cast into a gas chamber; each person being sent to be a prisoner in a concentration camp. Despit various attempts from different characters to ignore the state of the world, none could escape it.

One of the more well-known endings is from the 1993 Broadway revival, where the Emcee is reciting the lyrics from the opening song, "Willkommen". He asks the audience, "where are your troubles now? Forgotten? I told you so," and also states "life is beautiful. The girls are beautiful. Even the orchestra, is beautiful." Through the Kit Kat Klub's shows and fun, they still maintain that there are no troubles, and that everything is beautiful, except that nothing is beautiful, and the troubles have invaded the club. By the time the song is almost over, the Emcee is taking off his jacket with a knowing, and even happy smile, which falls away in an instant as he is revealed to be wearing a stripped jacket with a yellow star, red star, and pink triangle underneath.
